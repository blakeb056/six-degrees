Your connections export loses company and job title in December 2026

The platform's data download is dropping your connections' email addresses, current companies and job titles in December 2026. Download yours before then and keep it. Here is what still works afterwards, in Sixgree and elsewhere.

26 November 20263 min readBy Blake Burford

The platform's Get a copy of your data page now carries a notice: from December 2026, the download no longer includes your connections' email addresses, current companies or job titles. The notice names no day, so treat November 30 as the deadline.

If you have ever wanted a list of who you know and where they work, download it this week.

What changes

Today, Connections.csv has seven columns: first name, last name, profile link, email address (only when that person allows it), company, position, and the date you connected.

After the change it is expected to keep only:

  • first name and last name
  • the profile link
  • the date you connected

The notice lists what is removed. It does not show the new file, so the list of what stays is what the notice implies, not something anyone has seen yet. We'll update this post when the first December downloads arrive.

The notice also says you can still see what your connections share on their own profiles. That's true, but it means opening profiles one at a time instead of having one file.

What to do before December

  1. Request your connections export now. The steps take about ten minutes, on a computer or a phone: How to export your network connections.
  2. When the email arrives, download the zip and keep it somewhere safe, with your other important files.
  3. Write the date on it. A file downloaded now is a record of where your connections worked in November 2026.

A file you have already downloaded is yours. The change affects new downloads, not files on your computer.

What this means in Sixgree

A file downloaded before the change works in full. Sixgree reads it on your computer and ranks everyone by role and company in tiers S to D. It keeps the import in your data folder, so you can open it again any time, even after the platform stops sending those columns.

A file downloaded after the change can still be imported, with names, links and dates. But Sixgree's ranking is built on role and company, so without them it can't tell your connections apart: everyone gets the same score. Jobs and the company views need companies too.

Scanning can fill them in. The Auto scan and Manual scan plugins, from Settings → Plugins, read each person's headline from the platform's own pages, and with Dig deeper their current and past roles. That is the only way Sixgree has to fill in a network without the columns. It runs your own account automatically, slowly and within a daily limit, and the platform may restrict accounts that automate it; the risk is yours. Read The rules and the risk of scanning before you turn it on.

If you have both, use both: import the file you downloaded before December, then scan to add who your connections know.

Why it matters

The export was the one safe way to get your network out in a form you could sort, search and keep. Names and links alone are an address book without the addresses. Who you know is only half the picture; where they work is what turns it into a list of companies you can get into, and people who can introduce you.

That half is still yours if you download it this month.

Every person named in this post is invented: they're from the sample network that ships with Sixgree. Something wrong? Open an issue.
