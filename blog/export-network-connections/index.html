How to export your network connections, on a computer or an iPhone

Download your network connections as a CSV from the platform's own settings, on Mac, Windows or iPhone, what the file contains and what it leaves out, and how to see the people in it as a map with tiers.

6 October 20262 min readBy Blake Burford

You can export your network connections from the platform's own settings, on a computer or a phone, in about ten minutes. You get a file called Connections.csv with each connection's name, company, position and the date you connected. It's the platform's official export, so it carries no risk to your account.

From December 2026 the export leaves out company, position and email address. Download yours before then and keep it: what changes, and what still works.

On a computer (Mac or Windows)

  1. Open the platform's website, click your photo, then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Open Data privacy, then Get a copy of your data.
  3. Choose Want something in particular? and tick Connections only. That's the quick one.
  4. Click Request archive and confirm with your password.
  5. The platform emails you a download link, usually within about ten minutes. Download the zip and open it: Connections.csv is inside.

On an iPhone

  1. In the platform's app, tap your photo, then Settings.
  2. Tap Data privacy, then Get a copy of your data.
  3. Pick Connections only and tap Request archive.
  4. When the email arrives, open the link in Safari and download the zip. The Files app opens it; Connections.csv is inside.

If the app sends you to a web page for any step, finish there: it's the same setting.

What the file has, and what it doesn't

The CSV lists your first-degree connections: first and last name, profile link, company, position, the date you connected, and an email address only when that person allows it (most don't). From December 2026, new downloads are expected to keep only the names, profile link and date. It does not include who they know. Your 2nd and 3rd degree, the bridges between people and the shortest way in to someone, aren't in any export from the platform.

Turning the file into a map

Sixgree reads a Connections.csv on your own computer and shows every person in the Galaxy, ranked in tiers S to D, with nothing uploaded. That covers your first degree straight away. To see who your connections can introduce you to, Sixgree's scanner reads their circles too, slowly and within a daily limit; see Scanning slowly and safely and Network connection degrees explained.

A version that reads your CSV right here on sixgree.com, in your browser and on your phone, is on the roadmap.

Every person named in this post is invented: they're from the sample network that ships with Sixgree. Something wrong? Open an issue.
