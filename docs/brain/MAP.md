# Map — what lives where

## Application

| Path | Role |
|---|---|
| `app/page.js` | The main view. Owns view switching, tier filters, data-source selection, and the empty state. |
| `app/components/views.js` | **The view registry.** One row per visual: its component, which modes it belongs to, and any exception it needs. `page.js` and `FilterPanel` both read it, so the menu can never offer a view that does not exist. |
| `app/components/OrbitGraph.js` | Orbit — tier orbits with each bridge's circle fanned beside them as dots. A request you've sent rings that person's dots (dashed gold), in step with `lib/requests-client.js`, without rebuilding the scene. |
| `app/components/TheirCircle.js` | Their circle, at the top of a 1st-degree person's card: their scanned circle (D2), who you added from it (green ring) with their circles fanning out behind them, on out to D6; a request still out is a dotted dot. | `app/components/ForceGraph.js` | The Galaxy, Network Circle's force layout (~460 lines): the simulation, hover cards, and the selection ring, which follows `selectedId` and pulses on its own element over the `<svg>`. | `app/components/ChainView.js` | Bridge Chains — your bridges round you (on touch, a drag round the circle picks one), each with an "N ready" badge. | `app/insights/page.js` | Forwards an old link to Insights in the profile: `/insights` → People, `/insights?view=<board>` → that board (`lib/insights-address.js movedInsights`). It was the Insights tab, 0.8.0 to 1.0.0. |
| `app/components/insights/*` | Profile → ✦ Insights. `InsightsBoards` loads the network the way every page does (`loadCsvNetwork`, else `/api/network`, the scanner's notes, and the daily budget from `/api/scraper?usage`, which only reads files), works out the boards with `lib/insights-board.js` (companies and titles a moment after the table, since they read every headline), shows the board the profile asks for and reports each board's count for the notch. Health is `NetworkHealthSection`. `PeopleView` is the Power Index (mock-up B): filters, the ranked table a hundred rows at a time (25 on a phone) with a band for a big tie, and the rail (`Rail`: Untapped, Kingmakers, Gatekeepers, Hidden giants, Company power, How sure, Richest?). `Boards` holds the four full boards, `Report` the scrolling report (mock-up C), `parts` the chips, cards and actions: Open circle (`/?chain=<id>`), Scan circle (`/setup?scan=<id>`), Ask (the card's Connect: their profile in a new tab and `markRequested` through the best way in). |
| `app/components/CompanyScores.js` | The Scores tab: every company with its score, where the score comes from, and the control to set your own. It's on the Scores tab (`/scores`); the old `/paths?tab=scores` forwards there. |
| `app/components/LegacyScoresCard.js` | The card at the top of the Scores tab, shown once to a database scored with the curated list before it was made neutral: keep all, choose or no thanks (`/api/company-scores/legacy`). Hidden while a CSV import or the sample is open. |
| `app/components/OutlinkQuest.js` | Outlink → Circles: working through each mapped circle five people at a time (rules in `lib/quest.js`). |
| `app/paths` `app/queue` `app/import` `app/setup` `app/launch` | Secondary screens. Every one but the launch page has `AppHeader` and a notch: Paths' Map · Companies, Outlink's four, Scan's and Import's one tab. |
| `app/components/ScanStatusBar.js`, `lib/island.js`, `app/components/useNotchTabs.js` | The notch, mounted once in `app/layout.js`: hangs from the header's bottom edge, centred under `#main-tabs`, following the header up a page that scrolls and then waiting at the window's top. It holds the page's buttons and what's running. A page sets its buttons with `useNotchTabs` (a layout effect, so the next page's replace the last page's in the same frame); one tab is a notch too, lit; `divided` starts a second group (Profile's boards). Every page has one. |
| `app/settings/page.js` | Settings: Updates (`UpdatePanel`), a line pointing to LinkedIn usage on the Scan page, Appearance, Scores, Your data and About this copy, a `<Section>` per feature that adds a setting. The same header as every page; the sections are its notch, a pick scrolls to one and the one you're reading is lit. Reached from the ⚙ button and *Six Degrees → Settings…* (⌘,). |
| `app/components/settings/SectorSection.js` | Scores → Your sector: up to three picks (`SectorPicker`, with "Suggested from your network" from `/api/settings/sector-suggestions`), lean/strong, a dry run of what would change (`/api/settings/sector-preview`), and Save, which rescores everyone. Says when a CSV or the sample is open, since those aren't re-weighted. |
| `app/components/settings/UsageSection.js` | Scan → LinkedIn usage (`/setup#usage`, the Scan page's last section, linked from the notch's budget and the budget box; `/settings#usage` forwards there): a status pill, bars for the last 24 hours (marks at 50, 100 and 373), this month (LinkedIn's Pacific reset, the 250 to 350 people report shaded), the last 7 days against Auto scan's 200, and profile views; speed, about how many people are left to map, the last pushback, Auto scan's rules, a warning with *Back to 50 a day, 250 a month*. Reads `GET /api/scraper?usage`; every number says where it came from. |
| `app/components/settings/SectorPicker.js` | The sector picks, shared by Scores → Your sector and the Scan page's field question: your picks, a search box, and the twelve industries, each opening to its sectors from the directory. Picks only; the page saves. |
| `app/components/FieldStep.js` | The Scan page's one question, once your connections are in (step 3) and before who they know: your field, with `SectorPicker`, then *Continue* (which rescores everyone) or *Skip for now*, held at the foot of the window while the card runs past it, under "Your galaxy is ready". Either saves `fieldAsked`, so it's asked once. `FieldAnswer` is the line in its place afterwards. |
| `app/components/ui.js` | Shared pieces for Settings (`Section`, `Body`, `Mono`, `Status`, `Btn`). New screens use these rather than a private copy. |
| `app/components/InlineNote.js` | The line beside a scan button that says why it didn't start, and fades by itself (`useFadingNote`). It replaced every `alert()` and `window.confirm()` a scan start used to raise (Blake, 2026-10-04: no pop-ups). Every Scan button outside a card starts with `startHere()` (`lib/scraper-client.js`) and shows its answer in one of these. |
| `app/components/settings/DataSection.js` | Settings → Your data: the data folder (path, *Copy*, *Show in Finder*), what it takes up, the backups ("Last backup: <time>, verified", *Back up now*, and *Restore* and *Show in Finder* on each), and moving the network to another computer (*Export backup file…*, *Restore from a file…*, restart to finish). Only asks the server, on a click; the work is in `lib/backups.js` and `lib/data-*.js`. |
| `lib/settings.js` | What the user chose, as one JSON object in `app_meta` 'settings', so it travels with the data (and with a backup or a restore: the look, `theme`, and the Galaxy's saved layouts, `galaxyLayouts`, live here too, with the browser's localStorage as the copy read before the page paints: `lib/synced-setting.js`, `app/components/ThemeLoader.js`). Each setting declares a default and a `parse()`; undeclared keys are refused. A save rewrites only the keys it changes, so an older copy never erases a newer one's settings. |
| `lib/settings-client.js` | The browser's one way to save settings (`saveSettings`, `POST /api/settings`): Scores → Your sector and Tiers, and the Scan page's field question. |
| `lib/settings-effects.js` | What saving a setting sets in motion (a new sector focus rescores everyone and counts who moved, as the preview does). Each changed setting's work runs even if another's fails. Kept apart from the store because rescoring reads the settings: the store importing it would go in a circle. |
| `app/api/*` | 33 routes. See [`ENDPOINTS.md`](ENDPOINTS.md). |
| `app/api/data/*` | Settings → Your data: `GET /api/data` (the folder's facts and the backups, open) and the gated `export`, `import`, `restart`, `reveal`, `backup`, `restore`, and `csv` (a CSV import kept in the data folder: `lib/csv-store.js`). |
| `app/api/scraper/route.js` | Spawns the scraper on the app's behalf, so no second terminal or second server is needed. Runs it on the Python `lib/scanner-python.js` picks (the Mac app's own first), and sets the scanner up: Install, or Set up the scanner (download a pinned Python first). |
| `app/setup/page.js` | The Scan page, top to bottom (Blake, 2026-10-04): the scanner and, beside it, **Setup** as green lights, then **Scanner settings**, **Extras**, and **LinkedIn usage** (`UsageSection`, `#usage`). The scanner card: what's mapped, what's running with Stop and *Watch it fill in* (`/?chain=<id>`), a failed run's reason, *Your connections* (Scan my network, then Check for new / Scan it all again), *Their circles* (`ScanRadar` with speed and today's budget, this round, start with, *Why so slow?*), *In progress* (`PausedList` in `LinkedInLimits.js`, only while someone is stopped partway: Resume each, Resume all), *Save photos* while some photos are still links (`photosWaiting`), and the log. Setup: Scanner and Chrome (step 1, `lib/scanner-setup.js`), App Management (optional, amber, on a Mac), Scanning risks (the one-time *I understand*), LinkedIn sign-in. A green step is one line; a red one is open; every step has a chevron. *All set* once every step is green; until then the scan buttons say what's left, inline, and on a narrow window Setup comes first. Settings: the budget (`BudgetBox`), how much of each list, finish stopped lists, *Retry hidden ones*, the pacing in words. Extras: Auto scan (the experimental all-day switch, `lib/experimental-client.js`), Show the scanner's Chrome window, and the Social tab's daily messages sync once there are messages. Your field (`FieldStep`, when `askForField` says so) sits above it all. `/setup?scan=<id>` adds *Scan one circle* for that person: the cost, the day's budget, and a button (the id, read with `useSearchParams`: TRAPS §41). |
| `middleware.js` | Refuses requests addressed to another name, then cross-site writes, on all of `/api` and `/avatars`, then applies the destructive-route gate (`lib/gate.js requestRefusal`). Leaves out exactly `/api/data/import`, whose handlers make the same checks themselves before reading the upload (ENDPOINTS.md, "Request bodies over 10 MB"). |

## Library

| Path | Role |
|---|---|
| `lib/db.js` | **The keystone.** A Supabase-shaped query builder over `node:sqlite`. |
| `lib/db-client.js` | The one database handle (`getDb()`), the data folder, the schema step, and the backup before a new version. At the start, before it opens anything, `getDb()` finishes an import staged in `import-pending/` (`lib/data-import.js`), once per server process. |
| `lib/photos.js` | **Photos are shown from this computer only.** `localPhoto()` is the one check every view and the routes that store photos make, and the `/avatars` route serves the names it allows: a saved file's path (`/avatars/<name>`, `AVATAR_FILE`), or null, so a person with a link (what older versions stored) shows initials and browsing never loads anything from LinkedIn. `waitingPhotos()` lists those links for the scanner to save once each (`GET /api/update-images`). No imports; the views use it. The `img-src` Content-Security-Policy in `next.config.mjs` backs it up in the browser. |
| `lib/data-folder.js` | The data folder: what the Settings page shows about it (sizes from `lstat` only; `chrome-profile/` as there or not), the allow-list of what travels (`TRAVELLING_FILES` = `NETWORK_FILES`, replaced by an import, + `BUDGET_FILES`, merged; `AVATAR_FILE`, from `lib/photos.js`, shared with the `/avatars` route), opening it in Finder, and sweeping the private working folders a stopped run leaves (an export's, an upload's, Set up the scanner's) once an hour old, at each start and before the next run. Never follows a link, even one standing in for `avatars/`. |
| `lib/backups.js` | Backups in `backups/`: `makeBackup(kind)` (daily, before-update, manual) through `buildExport`, written to a hidden `.partial`, synced, checked by the importer (`validateImport`) and only then renamed, so a listed backup would restore; never the Social tab's files (`BACKUP_SOCIAL`: *Forget it* must forget). The rotation (`KEEP`: 7 daily, 3 before-update, 3 before-import never under 30 days old, never one made by hand), the daily check (at the start and after a scan, no timers), what the page shows (`backupReport`: the newest checked once per file per server), and restoring one (`restoreBackup`, through `stageImport`, by a name from the folder's own listing only). |
| `lib/galaxy-layouts.js`, `lib/synced-setting.js` | The Galaxy's saved layouts as a setting (`cleanLayouts`), and the rule for a setting kept with the network and cached in the browser: the network's wins; a network with none takes the browser's once. |
| `lib/data-export.js` | Builds the `.sixdegrees` file: `VACUUM INTO`, then the manifest and the allow-listed files with their SHA-256 ([`SCHEMA.md`](SCHEMA.md)). Only the photos a row still points at. |
| `lib/data-import.js` | Checks an upload before reading it (`admitImport`) and the file after (untrusted: SECURITY.md), rebuilds it into this version's schema in `import-pending/`, and swaps it in at the next start, step by step (a journal on the disk), after keeping what was there in `backups/`: never while another process has the database open, and only once the kept copy is synced and checks out. `RETIRED` lists names older exports may still have. Also what the page says about restarting. |
| `lib/durable.js` | Writes that must be on the disk before the next step counts on them: sync a file, sync a folder (so a rename is), write a file whole or not at all. Node's fsync is F_FULLFSYNC on a Mac. |
| `lib/linkedin-limits.js` | The LinkedIn search budget, the cap on profile views and the cooldown, read with the scanner's rules; the Scan page's two edits (lifting a cooldown keeps it, marked `lifted_at`, its `until` moved to the moment it was lifted, so every reader sees it ended); Scan → LinkedIn usage's closer reading (`linkedinUsage`: the last hour and 7 days, when the next search or profile view frees, the last pushback from the cooldown file and the newest `pushback/*.txt`, its time and reason only); and an import's budget files, checked (`budgetFileProblem`) and merged (`mergeBudgetFiles`), since they belong to the account. |
| `lib/search-risk.js` | When a search budget is risky (over 100 a day, or no monthly cap) and what the budget picker asks and says about it. No Node imports, so the page and tests share it. |
| `lib/usage.js` | Scan → LinkedIn usage's rules: Auto scan's own ceilings (`AUTO`, checked against `scrape.py` by `tests/usage.test.mjs`), about 9 people a search, the level (`usageLevel`: paused, too close at 60% of 373 or searching within a day of a pushback, risky over 100, above the default over 50, ok), its plain-word warning, what's left (`estimate`), and the times in words. No Node imports. |
| `lib/auto-scan.js` | Auto scan's rules (experimental): its paces (Slow, Medium, Fast: a sitting's size and the rest after it), tiers, hours, `autoPlan` (go, hours, rest, stop), the state in words. ## Data directory

`SIX_DEGREES_HOME`, default `~/.six-degrees`:

```
six-degrees.sqlite        the database (WAL mode)
avatars/                  permanent WebP copies of profile photos
chrome-profile/           the scraper's browser profile — holds a live session
backups/                  daily-, before-update- and manual-<version>-<time>.sixdegrees
                          (7, 3 and all kept), before-import-<time>.sqlite + -files/ (3,
                          never one under 30 days old), and auto-before-<version>-*.sqlite
                          from older versions (counted with before-update)
import-pending/           an import waiting for the next start (READY, data.sqlite, files/)
csv-network.json          a LinkedIn CSV import, kept until × removes it (lib/csv-store.js)
*.json                    the scanner's progress, skip lists, budget and cooldown;
                          signed-in.json, whether it last found you signed in (this
                          computer's Chrome only, so it never travels)
venv/ pushback/           the scanner's Python add-ons; LinkedIn page dumps
python/                   a Python for them that Set up the scanner downloaded (not in the Mac app, which carries its own)
```

What an export carries is an allow-list (`lib/data-folder.js`): the database, `avatars/`
and the six scanner files, and a kept CSV import. Never `chrome-profile/`, `venv/`, `python/`, `backups/`, `pushback/`,
`app-version` or anything temporary.

**`chrome-profile/` grants access to the user's LinkedIn account.** Treat it like a
password. It is the one artifact here worth protecting.
