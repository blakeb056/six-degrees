Get started

Bring your network in

The app opens on three ways in, from no risk at all to the full picture. Scanning is the only one that shows who your connections know.

Explore a sample

No LinkedIn risk

Import LinkedIn's export

LinkedIn's own Connections.csv, read on your computer: Settings & Privacy → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data → Connections. It shows the people you know, not who they know, so there's no Degrees or Outlink.

Your account, your risk

Scan your LinkedIn

The only way to see who your connections know. The app opens Google Chrome with a profile of its own, you sign into LinkedIn there yourself, and it reads slowly with daily limits.

Scanning uses your own LinkedIn account, and LinkedIn may restrict accounts that automate it. Automating LinkedIn may break its User Agreement. The scanner reads slowly, keeps to a budget and stops at the first push-back, but the risk is yours. The sample and the CSV import carry no LinkedIn risk at all. How scanning works.

Your data

One folder, on your computer

Your network lives in a hidden folder in your home folder, .six-degrees. Open it any time with Help → Show the Data Folder, and see what each part takes up in Settings → Your data.

  • Backups. Six Degrees backs up your network, photos and all, into backups/ once a day while it's open, before each new version first opens it, and before an import or a restore. Each one is checked as it's made. Settings → Your data shows the last one and puts any of them back with Restore.
  • Moving to a new computer. Settings → Your data → Export backup file… makes one file. Move it yourself, and restore it on the other computer. Your LinkedIn sign-in never goes into it.
  • The one thing to guard. chrome-profile/ holds the scanner's signed-in LinkedIn session. Never copy, sync or share it; deleting it just means signing in again.

Every detail, including how to undo an import, is in the README.

Privacy

What it contacts, and when

  • No account, no server. Everything stays on your computer: your network (and an imported CSV, until you remove it) in the hidden .six-degrees folder, and the app window's own storage in ~/Library/Application Support/Six Degrees.
  • Nothing is uploaded. No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports.
  • It only goes online when you act: LinkedIn while you scan or press Auto (profile photos are saved on your computer as they're read, and shown only from there), and GitHub when you check for updates (and to download one, if you click Install). The Mac app carries the scanner's Python inside it, so setting it up downloads nothing.
  • One thing that runs on a timer, if you turn it on: the experimental daily messages sync (Outlink → Messages & follow-ups) opens LinkedIn once a day, in the daytime, while the app is open. It's off until you tick it.
  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub, under the MIT licence, and SECURITY.md describes what the app trusts.

This website has no trackers either. To show the latest version and the number of stars, it asks GitHub.

Questions

Questions people ask

Is Six Degrees free?
Yes. It's free and open source under the MIT licence, with no account, no subscription and no paid tier. Every line of code is on GitHub.
Does it upload my LinkedIn data anywhere?
No. Your network stays on your Mac, in a hidden .six-degrees folder. There's no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reports. It only goes online when you act: LinkedIn while you scan or press Auto, and GitHub when you check for an update.
Does it work on Windows?
Yes, in beta: a one-click Setup.exe for Windows 10 or 11, the same app as on the Mac with the scanner's Python inside (how). There's a Linux app too (.deb or .tar.gz), and on Linux it still runs with npx six-degrees as well.
Why does macOS say it can't check the app for malware?
Because the app isn't signed with Apple's paid developer certificate yet. Allow it once in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway, or use the one-line Terminal install, which checks the download against the published checksums before opening it.
Is scanning LinkedIn against LinkedIn's rules?
Automating LinkedIn may break its User Agreement, and LinkedIn can restrict an account for it. Scanning is optional, slow on purpose, kept to a daily budget, and stops at the first push-back. The sample network and LinkedIn's own CSV export carry no risk to your account.
What do I need to get started?
A Mac and, optionally, LinkedIn's Connections.csv export (Settings → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data). Or nothing: a sample network of 150 invented people ships with the app, so you can explore every view before importing anything.
What does “six degrees” mean here?
The idea that anyone is reachable through a short chain of people who know each other. The app draws your chain: who you know, who they know, and the shortest introduction path from you to someone you haven't met.
How is someone's score worked out?
From their title and their company: power = title × (0.45 + 0.055 × company score), plus a small bonus for reach and for a strong circle. It estimates network position, not what anyone is worth as a person. Scanned and sample people show the working on their card.
