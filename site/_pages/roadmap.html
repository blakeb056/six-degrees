Roadmap

Where it's going

Started in June 2026, open source since August, released in public since September, and 1.0 since October. What's shipped, what's next, and what's being tested. No dates for what's next: things ship when they work, and every release is in the release notes.

Next

  • Windows and Linux out of betaBoth apps are a beta today. Once the Windows one has been opened on a real Windows PC, they become full releases.
  • A signed Windows appSo SmartScreen stops asking, as the signed Mac app opens without a warning. Until then Windows stays an unsigned beta.
  • Try Sixgree in your browserA lite version of the app on invented people: every tab open, build bridges and watch new people join. No download, no account, no real scanning.
  • Your LinkedIn export, in tiers, on this siteDrop LinkedIn's Connections.csv onto sixgree.com and see your people in the Galaxy. It's read in your browser only: the page isn't allowed to upload anything. Works on a phone too.
  • What changed since your last scanWho's new, whose circle grew, and who changed jobs.
  • Clusters and communitiesGroups in your network, found from the ties between your connections that circle scans already keep.

Being tested

  • Auto-Bridge and Auto scanAll-day pacing, tried on real accounts before it loses its experimental label.
  • The live messages syncReading your messages list in your own Chrome, once a day (Outlink → Messages & follow-ups). Not yet tried on a live account.
  • Company and bridge scansRe-checked end to end against LinkedIn's current pages.
  • The Intel Mac buildOpened and checked on an Intel Mac, not just built for one.
  • Searching only your 2nd degreeFewer searches for the same picture.
  • Gentler pacingVaried pauses, short breaks and natural scrolling on every page, layered on top of the scanner that works today, with a switch to turn it off.
  • Full profile readsOptional and off by default: past roles from each profile, counted against your daily searches and at least a minute apart.
  • Strategy engineExperimental and off by default: who gatekeeps whom in your network, the people you can only reach through one person, and how many industries they span. A separate lens; tiers don't change.

How it started

Three generations in about three months.

  1. 11 June 2026The first build. "6 Degrees of Separation": a LinkedIn network research tool with a force-directed graph, hosted online. Archive: the original prototype (June 2026), kept as it was.
  2. 12–15 July 2026The static version. Your LinkedIn network as a galaxy, with leverage tiers, bridges and introduction paths. Local-first and entirely static: the first version where your data stayed on your device.
  3. 21 August 2026The open-source rebuild. This codebase: a desktop app with a local database, no account and no keys.
  4. 24 September 2026The first public release, 0.1.0, then the 0.4 releases through late September. Every release.

What won't change

  • It runs on your computerNo account, no server, no telemetry.
  • It's free and openMIT licence, every line on GitHub, no paid tier.
  • Scanning stays slowBudgets, rests and a stop at the first push-back. The risk is always spelled out.
  • Honest numbersYour own network, never a percentile against anyone else's.
