About

Everyone is a few introductions away.

Sixgree turns the professional network you already have into a map of the people you can reach, and who can get you there. It runs on your own computer, and it's open source.

The vision

The most useful thing in a professional network isn't the list of people you know. It's the people they know, and which of your connections is the way to each of them. That picture exists in every network, but no one can see it: a list of connections shows one layer, one person at a time.

Sixgree draws it. Who can introduce you to someone, ranked. Who only one of your connections can reach. How much of your network hangs on a single person. And the shortest path to someone you haven't met. The aim is a tool anyone can use to understand their network, without handing that network to anyone.

The problem

Your network is invisible

You can't see who your connections know, so the warmest introduction is usually one you never think to ask for.

Lists don't show structure

A spreadsheet or a contacts list has no idea that two of your connections open the same doors, or that one of them is your only way into a whole circle.

Your network is personal

It's other people's names and jobs. Sending it to another online service to analyse is a real cost, and many people won't.

Why now

The data is yours to take

Anyone can download their own connections as a file, from their networking platform's Get a copy of your data page. Sixgree reads that file with no risk to your account.

A laptop is enough

Laying out and scoring the platform's maximum of 30,000 connections runs on your own computer, with no server. It's in the release notes for 0.2.1.

Network science, made plain

Ideas from social-network research, like brokerage and effective reach, turned into plain answers: who only they can reach.

What makes it different

  • Local-firstNo account, no server, no telemetry. Your network never leaves the computer it's on. Why.
  • Open source, MITEvery line is on GitHub. Anyone can read, check, change or build on it.
  • Honest by designA fair comparison, the scanner's risk in plain words, and your numbers never ranked against anyone else's.
  • It answers "who do I ask?"Not a chart to admire: a ranked list of introductions, and a game for asking for them.

Open source, on purpose

Sixgree is released under the MIT licence: use it, change it and share it, for anything, as long as the copyright notice goes with it. A tool that reads your network should be one you can inspect. The code, the scoring model, the tests and the roadmap are all public, and contributions are welcome.
