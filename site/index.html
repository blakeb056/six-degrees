The problem

Your network is bigger than you can see.

Every person you know knows people you don't. The introduction you need is usually one step away, but a list of connections can't tell you who it runs through.

Six Degrees draws the whole shape, and shows you the way in.

150 connections in the sample network that ships with the app lead to 598 people one introduction away.

01 See your network

Everyone you know. And everyone they know.

Everyone, at a glance.

Network Circle puts your connections on rings around you, the ones who can open the most doors closest in. Prefer a pyramid or a ranked list? One click.

Then everyone they know.

Scan a connection's circle and their people join the picture, a band further out, each one next to the person you'd go through.

02 Find your way in

The shortest path to anyone.

Who can introduce you. Ranked.

Separation lists everyone two steps away, strongest first, with every connection who knows them. So you know exactly who to ask.

Follow the chain.

Bridge Chains opens any connection's circle, then circles inside circles, as far as your scans reach. Orbit, one of the Galaxy's layouts, fans them out behind the person they came through.

Every connection, explained.

Open anyone's card to see their circle, how many people only they can reach, who opens the same doors, and where their people work.

Introductions, as a game.

Outlink offers each connection's best five people at a time, and the next best moves. Mark an invite as sent to fill the ring and unlock five more. Points come from introductions you ask for, never from browsing.

03 Know your network

Where you're strong, and where one person is your only door.

Your network, ranked by power.

The Power Index, under Insights in your profile, ranks everyone within two steps of you, each person once. Kingmakers have the most powerful people behind them, Gatekeepers are the ones your reach hangs on, and Untapped counts the top-tier people you haven't asked yet. Every row ends in a next step: open their circle, ask, or Auto.

Richest? It never sees anyone's money, and says so. Power is the honest measure it has.

See who's your only way in.

Some people you can reach through one connection and no one else. Network health shows how much of your network you can reach more than one way, and the five connections who reach the most people no one else does.

Only your own numbers. Never a ranking against anyone else.

Who's warm. Who's waiting.

Outlink's Messages & follow-ups reads LinkedIn's own export of your data: who you're in touch with, who's waiting on a reply, and the connections each chapter of your career brought. It keeps dates and counts, never what a message says.

04 Watch it grow

Your network, in motion.

experimental

A physics lab for your galaxy.

Obsidian's forces, center, repel, link force and link distance, saying what each does to your network. Pick Clusters and it draws like Obsidian: one round disc, each scanned connection a big hub with their circle around them, and anyone in two circles tying them together. Turn up Orbit and it all slowly turns round you.

Colour it. Find anyone.

Colour the Galaxy by tier, degree, company or warmth. Type a name, a company or a role, and everyone who matches lights up. Names on, or off.

Watch it grow.

Replay your network by the day you made each connection, with your job changes marked along the way. Save it as a video, or the Galaxy as a picture.

05 Grow it, carefully

A scanner that takes its time.

Slow on purpose.

The optional scanner reads your own LinkedIn in a Chrome window you sign into yourself, in four steps with one button each. It keeps to a daily budget, rests between pages, and stops at the first sign of push-back. Pick Slow, Medium or Fast: Fast is the pace it has always had, the others only add waiting. The notch under the header shows what it's doing on every page, and the In progress list carries on where each scan stopped. It reads; the one thing it sends is a connection request, without a note, when you press Auto on someone.

Automating LinkedIn may break its User Agreement, and LinkedIn can restrict accounts that do it. The risk is yours. LinkedIn's own export and the sample network carry none. How scanning works.

experimental

Auto scan, and a quiet window.

All-day pacing that rests between sittings and searches only in the daytime, with one Auto scan button to start it. And an option to hide the Chrome window, with its risks spelled out right beside it.

06 Yours alone

Private by design. Open to the last line.

It never leaves your computer.

No account. No server. No telemetry. Your network is one folder on your computer, and the app goes online only when you ask it to: LinkedIn while you scan or press Auto, GitHub when you check for an update.

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT licence, so you can check exactly what it does.
