01 Why Six Degrees

Your network is bigger than your list of connections

A connections list tells you who you know. Six Degrees shows what that network can do: who can introduce you to whom, where one connection is your only way in, and how it all grew.

Degrees → Separation

Who can introduce you, ranked

Everyone two steps away, ranked, each with every way in: which of your connections knows them, and how many do.

Bridge Chains · a card's Insights

Who only one connection reaches

For each connection whose circle you've scanned, the people none of your other connections reach. Show them lists exactly who.

Scores → Network health

Your network's health

How much of your 2nd degree you reach two or more ways, your effective reach, and the five connections who reach the most people no one else does. Only your own numbers, never a percentile against anyone.

Galaxy → Filters · experimental

A physics lab, and a replay

Sliders for gravity, rings, push and pull. Colour by tier, degree, company or warmth. Replay your network growing by the day you connected, and save it as a picture or a video.

Everywhere

All on your own computer

No account, no server, no telemetry. Your network is a folder in your home folder, and the app goes online only when you ask it to.

02 Features

What it shows you

Network Circle

Everyone you know on rings by how much they can open up for you, the most powerful closest to you.

Degrees

The people two steps away, ranked, each with every way in: which of your connections can introduce you. Needs a scan, or try it with the sample.

Paths

Your network by company and industry: who you know at each company, who you can reach, and the warmest way in.

Outlink

Getting introduced, as a game: work through each connection's circle five people at a time. Needs a scan.

Scores

How every power score is worked out, in one place, and the three things you can change: your field, how tiers are graded, and any company's score.

Scan

The guided scanner: its progress, the budget left today, a cooldown lock, and a Paused list to carry on from.

Social experimental

From your own LinkedIn data: who's warm, dormant or waiting on a reply, and your career chapters. Message text is never kept.

Updates and moving

Install and restart updates in one click, checked against the release's checksums. Save a copy of your network to take to another computer.

New in 0.4.5

A physics lab for the Galaxy

Sliders for gravity, rings, push, pull and distance, in the spirit of Obsidian's graph. Let the rings go and each connection pulls their circle round them.

Watch your network grow

Replay your connections by the day you made them, with a time slider, your job changes marked along it, and a video you can save.

Colour, find and names

Colour the Galaxy by tier, degree, company or warmth, light up a name or company, and switch names on or off.

05 Get started

Three steps to your galaxy

  1. 1 Install it
  2. 2 Bring your network in
  3. 3 Explore it

Step 1 of 3

Install it in a minute

Which download? Apple menu → About This Mac. "Chip: Apple M…" means Apple Silicon. "Processor: …Intel…" means Intel.

  1. Drag it into Applications

    Open the file you downloaded (Six-Degrees-Mac-….dmg, in your Downloads folder). In the window that opens, drag Six Degrees onto the Applications folder.

  2. Open it once, and close the warning

    Open Six Degrees from Applications. macOS says it can't check the app for malware, because it isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate yet. Close that message with Done (on macOS 13 or 14: OK or Cancel). Anything except Move to Trash.

  3. Allow it in System Settings

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to "Six Degrees" was blocked, and click Open Anyway. Confirm with Open Anyway again (on macOS 13 or 14: Open), and your password if asked. That's once per download; after that it opens like any app.

Or skip those steps with Terminal

Press ⌘ Space, type Terminal and press Return. Paste this line and press Return:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blakeb056/six-degrees/main/install.sh | bash

It downloads the right version for your Mac, checks the file is exactly the one published, puts Six Degrees in Applications and opens it. Running it again updates the app. Read the script.

Step 2 of 3

Bring your network in

Three ways, from no risk at all to the full picture. Scanning is the only one that shows who your connections know.

No LinkedIn risk

Explore a sample

150 invented connections and the 598 people they know. Every view except Outlink, which needs your own scan.

No LinkedIn risk

Import LinkedIn's export

LinkedIn's own Connections.csv, read on your Mac. It shows the people you know, not who they know (so no Degrees or Outlink), and there's no risk to your LinkedIn account.

Your account, your risk

Scan your LinkedIn

The only way to see who your connections know. The app opens Google Chrome with a profile of its own, you sign into LinkedIn there yourself, and it reads slowly with daily limits. Needs Google Chrome (in Applications); the scanner's Python comes with the app.

Scanning uses your own LinkedIn account, and LinkedIn may restrict accounts that automate it. Automating LinkedIn may break its User Agreement. It happened twice while building this app. The scanner now reads slowly, keeps to a budget and stops at the first push-back, but the risk is yours. The sample and the CSV import carry no LinkedIn risk at all.

On Linux

Run it on Linux today

Six Degrees runs on Linux from the Terminal with npx, which comes with Node.js: it downloads Six Degrees from npm, the public library of Node.js programs, and runs it in your browser. A Linux app to download is coming soon.

One command

Install Node 22.13 or later (from nodejs.org or nvm; Ubuntu's own nodejs package is too old), then run:

npx six-degrees

It opens http://127.0.0.1:6363 in your browser and keeps your data in ~/.six-degrees. Stop it with Ctrl-C; update it with npx six-degrees@latest. Scanning also needs Google Chrome (not Chromium), and Python 3.10 to 3.14 with venv, or the Scan page's Set up the scanner, which downloads a private copy with one click. Windows isn't supported yet.

06 Privacy

Your network stays on your computer

  • No account, no server. Everything stays on your computer: your network in a hidden .six-degrees folder, and the app window's own storage (including an imported CSV) in ~/Library/Application Support/Six Degrees.
  • Nothing is uploaded. No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reports.
  • It only goes online when you act: LinkedIn while you scan (profile photos are saved on your Mac as they're read, and shown only from there), and GitHub when you check for updates (and to download one, if you click Install). The scanner and its Python come inside the app, so setting it up downloads nothing.
  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub, under the MIT licence.

This page has no trackers either. To show the latest version number, it asks GitHub once.

Questions people ask

Is Six Degrees free?
Yes. It's free and open source under the MIT licence, with no account, no subscription and no paid tier. Every line of code is on GitHub.
Does it upload my LinkedIn data anywhere?
No. Your network stays on your Mac, in a hidden .six-degrees folder. There's no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reports. It only goes online when you act: LinkedIn while you scan, and GitHub when you check for an update.
Does it work on Windows?
Not yet. Six Degrees is a Mac app for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs on macOS 13.5 or later, and it runs on Linux with npx six-degrees. A Windows version is planned, without a date.
Why does macOS say it can't check the app for malware?
Because the app isn't signed with Apple's paid developer certificate yet. Allow it once in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway, or use the one-line Terminal install, which checks the download against the published checksums before opening it.
Is scanning LinkedIn against LinkedIn's rules?
Automating LinkedIn may break its User Agreement, and LinkedIn can restrict an account for it. Scanning is optional, slow on purpose, kept to a daily budget, and stops at the first push-back. The sample network and LinkedIn's own CSV export carry no risk to your account.
What do I need to get started?
A Mac and, optionally, LinkedIn's Connections.csv export (Settings → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data). Or nothing: a sample network of 150 invented people ships with the app, so you can explore every view before importing anything.
What does “six degrees” mean here?
The idea that anyone is reachable through a short chain of people who know each other. The app draws your chain: who you know, who they know, and the shortest introduction path from you to someone you haven't met.
