You can export your network connections from your network's own settings, on a computer or a phone, in about ten minutes. You get a file called **Connections.csv** with each connection's name, company, position and the date you connected. It's your network's official export, so it carries no risk to your account.

## On a computer (Mac or Windows)

1. Open the platform's website, click your photo, then **Settings & Privacy**.
2. Open **Data privacy**, then **Get a copy of your data**.
3. Choose **Want something in particular?** and tick **Connections** only. That's the quick one.
4. Click **Request archive** and confirm with your password.
5. The platform emails you a download link, usually within about ten minutes. Download the zip and open it: **Connections.csv** is inside.

## On an iPhone

1. In the platform's app, tap your photo, then **Settings**.
2. Tap **Data privacy**, then **Get a copy of your data**.
3. Pick **Connections** only and tap **Request archive**.
4. When the email arrives, open the link in Safari and download the zip. The Files app opens it; **Connections.csv** is inside.

If the app sends you to a web page for any step, finish there: it's the same setting.

## What the file has, and what it doesn't

The CSV lists your **first-degree** connections: first and last name, profile link, company, position, the date you connected, and an email address only when that person allows it (most don't). It does **not** include who *they* know. Your 2nd and 3rd degree, the bridges between people and the shortest way in to someone, aren't in any export from the platform.

## Turning the file into a map

Sixgree reads a Connections.csv on your own computer and shows every person in the Galaxy, ranked in tiers S to D, with nothing uploaded. That covers your first degree straight away.
