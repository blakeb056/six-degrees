2.10.2

27 November 2026

  • 3 changed
  • 1 security

On GitHub, with the downloads

The app and its scanner plugins no longer carry their code in readable form; security updates.

Changed

  • The app carries only what it runs. A download used to hold Sixgree's own code as written, notes and comments included. Now it holds only the compiled, minified app: nothing to read, and less to download.
  • Scanner plugins 1.1.0 come compiled. Auto scan and Manual scan 1.1.0 carry the scanner as compiled Python instead of its source, for Sixgree 2.10.2 and later. They scan exactly as before; Settings → Plugins offers the update. An older Sixgree keeps the scanner it has.
  • Scanning from a copy without the app's own Python needs Python 3.11 to 3.14. The app brings its own, so nothing changes there.

Security

  • Updated Next.js, Electron and the scanner's image library. Next.js 16.4.6, Electron 44.11.6, Pillow 13.0.0 and certifi 2026.11.20, each with published security fixes.

2.10.1

26 November 2026

  • 1 changed
  • 1 fixed

Sixgree is free to use and no longer open source; Jobs keeps everyone at a company however its name is written.

Changed

  • Sixgree is free to use, and no longer open source. From this version on, the code is private and Sixgree comes under its own licence: free to download and use, but not to copy, change, resell or take apart. Versions up to and including 2.10.0 stay under the MIT License for anyone who has them. Downloads, updates, the one-line install and sixgree.com work exactly as before. Sixgree is no longer published to npm: on Linux, use the .deb or .tar.gz.

Fixed

2.10.0

26 November 2026

  • 9 added
  • 2 changed
  • 2 fixed
  • 4 removed

Jobs: where someone can refer you, counting who used to work there; a scanner step after import; exports without titles say so; Breach hidden for now.

Added

  • Jobs counts who used to work there. Someone who left a company can't refer you, but can introduce you to their old team, so former employees now count too: half as much as the same person there now, less the longer ago they left, by the role they held there. Their past jobs come from profiles a scan read (Dig deeper, with dates: "2019–2023") or a headline's "Former PM at Acme" or "ex-Acme". A company you'd only reach through someone who left is listed too, marked Former only, with "Ask Kai Moreno, who used to work there, for an introduction". An open company lists Work there now and Used to work there side by side, with faces, as Breach does; the page now has the other tabs' width and headings.
  • Jobs, a tab of its own: where someone can refer you. Every company someone you know works at now, ranked by a referral score out of 100, with the one person to ask: "Ask Ada Quill for a referral", or "Ask Leo Park to introduce you to Ada Quill" for someone you'd reach through a connection. The score weighs how you know them, how close they sit to hiring, how strong the connection who'd introduce you is, and whether their role was read from their profile or only from their headline; How the score works says it in words. Add the companies you're applying to: they come first, and Open in Breach maps who works there.
  • After an import, the setup asks you to install a scanner. With no scanner yet, the import's result becomes Now fill in your network: the Auto scan and Manual scan cards with Install as the main button, each card's risk in sight, and Skip for now as a small link. Installing still shows what the plugin is before anything installs.
  • A connections export with no job titles or companies says so. From December 2026 the platform's export leaves them out. Such a file is still imported (names, profile links, the dates you connected), and the preview and the result say plainly that everyone gets the same score until a scan reads their headlines, and that an export from before December is better.
  • The learning log records how you use Sixgree, not only what you send. Kept on this computer only and never sent anywhere, so later models and analysis have real data to work from:
    • your sessions (open, close, active time, version, plugins, class, look, window size);
    • each tab and sub-tab, how long it was shown and what led there (the header, the notch, a link or a card);
    • filters, the Tiers switch, shapes, sorts, Map views, Show ties and searches. A search for a person keeps only its length, never the name; company and sector searches keep their words;
    • a circle's focus (click or hold, how long, how deep) and Bridge Chains' dives;
    • cards opened and for how long, profiles opened, Connect, and pauses of 1.5 s or more on a dot;
    • Separation's and Breach's sections folded, shown and seen;
    • every This or That duel shown as well as answered, and how the class list is browsed;
    • each scan's start, every page with its pace, breaks taken or cut short, limits, stops and errors;
    • the LinkedIn panel's sections, the time spent in each, and Back and Forward;
    • every setting you change;
    • errors, slow frames on Network Circle and failed requests, with nothing personal in them.
  • Every kind of event is documented in docs/brain/LEARNING-LOG.md, with its fields and when it fires, and the tests check that each one has something firing it.
  • Record my behaviour (local only) in Settings → Your data, on by default. Off, only your requests and how they went are kept, which the models need.
  • Your data shows counts per kind, and the log exports as JSON or as a CSV per kind (a zip).
  • Readers for analysis: session summaries, the funnel from card to accepted, time per tab, and This or That calibration (GET /api/learning?sessions=1, funnel=1, dwell=1, duels=1).

Changed

  • Breach is hidden for now; Jobs takes its place. The tab, its tour step, hint and how-to are gone, and Jobs is in the tour instead. Breach's page and code stay (Jobs reads a company the way Breach does), so it can come back. In Jobs, I'm applying here puts a company at the top.
  • The pages' events go in batches every few seconds and as the window closes, with repeats and floods dropped (and counted), so logging never slows the app.

Fixed

  • A connection with no position or company in the export no longer has their name read as a headline, where a first name such as Dean or Major could score as a job title.
  • The learning log's size in Settings left out every event that has no dedupe key.

Removed

  • Messages & follow-ups is gone. Its page (/social) and its CRM (stages, tags, notes and follow-up dates), the reader for your own data export, the Profile's card for it, the Scan page's once-a-day messages sync and the scanner's messages mode (--messages) are removed.
  • What read from it went too. The Galaxy's Warmth colouring, and the job starts and posts marked along the replay's timeline. A layout saved coloured by Warmth opens coloured by tier.
  • The Social option in Export backup file. A copy never carries those files now. A backup file exported before this still restores: its Social files are left out, not refused.
  • Nothing is deleted: the social-, social-messages- and crm- files it kept in your data folder stay where they are.

2.9.0

19 October 2026

  • 9 added
  • 9 changed
  • 8 fixed

Classes: pick your journey and Sixgree scores everyone for it, a learning log from day one, scan windows of 60 pages, Bridge Chains you can dive into, and Outlink retired.

Added

  • Classes. Pick a class for what you're after: Poacher (sales), Founder, Seeker (investors), Grower (your local circle), Cartographer (your network's structure) or Infiltrator (a job). Your class ranks the same network its own way, as a second lens: every person gets a class tier beside their network tier ("Class S · Network A"), and your network tiers stay exactly as they were. Each class card flips to show what it would find in your own network.
  • Name your target. One question per class: who you sell to and where, what you're building, what kind of capital, where you grow, or where you want to work (which also becomes your Breach targets), with an optional line about you.
  • This or That. About 16 quick duels, two cards side by side: pick the one you'd rather have, or say both are great, or neither. Each class asks its own questions: the Grower about sectors and kinds of people, the others about positions, seniority and companies. Cards mix real titles from your network with made-up ones where yours has no good example, and never show a name or a photo. The pairs are chosen to learn the most from each answer, it stops when your answers are enough, and keys work too (left, right, up for both, down for neither, space to skip, Z to undo). It runs while your first scan reads, where the field question was, and still sets your field when you hadn't picked one.
  • Never show me. Students, recruiters, sectors or companies you'd rather not see ranked: Less pushes them down, Never takes them off every class list.
  • Your build. What your answers weigh, as five bars (sector, role, place, company, reach), your new top 5 to say "more like this" or "not this one" to, and how many people moved into class S.
  • The Class tab. Your class, your build, the people it ranks highest (with Ask), and a map of class worth against network worth: act first, destinations, bridges, background.
  • Settings → Class. Switch class any time. Each class keeps its own answers, so switching back brings its scoring back exactly; a class with none asks its questions. Retune or retake one class without touching the others. Networks from before classes are offered the pick once.
  • The learning log. Sixgree now keeps a record, on this computer only, of who was shown where, what you opened, sent and skipped, how each request went (when it was sent and accepted, and how warm: within a day very warm, 3 days warm, 14 days cool, later cold), each scan's yield, your This or That answers and LinkedIn's load by the hour, so later versions can learn what works for you. Settings → Your data shows its size, exports it as JSON, and deletes it. It is never sent anywhere.
  • The send log. Every connection request now records when it was sent, how (a Connect, Auto or the LinkedIn panel), the person as known then, and every attempt, and an accept records the day it happened.

Changed

  • A send earns a token 1 XP, whoever it's to. Paying more for S-tier sends pushed toward sending and scanning more; accepted requests still carry the XP.
  • The places file can carry each city's time zone (scripts/build-places.mjs keeps GeoNames' zone column), for best-time-to-send later. The shipped file gains zones when it's next rebuilt.
  • Tier rings, and no dotted lines. Degrees → Unscanned was one giant disc of dots; it now rings everyone by tier round you: S (gold) nearest and biggest, then A, B and C, D furthest out, small and muted, with a gap and a letter for each tier and names under S and A once they fit. Bridge Chains' dive, its focus on a circle seen outside the scan only, and a circle built in Unscanned once it settles all use the same rings and dots. Your line to a circle stops at its edge instead of running dotted across it, and in Bridge Chains someone seen outside the scan is a lighter dot, not a dashed one.
  • Scan windows replace the daily search limit. Every search page counts in a window, by hand or automatic, your connections, Breach and Read all mutuals alike: at most 60 search pages a window, at most two windows a day, and a new window only 6 hours after the last one began, so 120 a day at most. Profile pages don't count in it. A scan stops when the window is used, saves what it read and says when the next window opens. The Scan page, the notch and the scan light show "Window 1 of 2: 34 of 60" and when the next window opens.
  • The windows are settings that only get stricter. Pages a window, windows a day and hours between windows are in Scanner settings, with 60, 2 and 6 as both the defaults and the most. Lifting the limits for a session and Dev mode put them back to those and never past them; Dev mode's old ceiling of 250 a day is gone. The breaks by hand and the speeds are as they were.
  • Profile views a day is the old daily number, now for profile views only (50 by default). Lifting the limits still takes it and the cooldown off for the session.
  • Outlink is gone. Its tab, the Circles game, the To add and Pending lists, Open All and Open Top 10, the Profile's Queue Progress card and the old /api/queue route are removed. Separation does the same job. Requests you marked sent stay marked everywhere.
  • Who they can introduce. On the Map and on a connection's card, the button that opened Outlink now shows Separation filtered to that connection's circle. The Map's chain card's Ask X for an intro opens X's profile, in Sixgree's own browser where it can.
  • Messages & follow-ups is a page of its own again (/social), under the same header as every page, with no tab: your Profile has a card for it, and the Scan page's daily messages sync links to it.

Fixed

  • A company scan no longer moves everyone's tiers. The curve's S and A cut-offs counted the people a company scan found, so one Breach scan of a big company shifted your whole network. They now come from your own connections only.
  • A company scan no longer lifts an unknown company's score. Its people counted toward the company's headcount in your network, raising it from 5 to 6. Only your 1st and 2nd degree count now.
  • Bridge Chains goes one level deeper. In a bridge's focus, click the person in the middle, or scroll or pinch in, and their circle opens full size: unblurred, filling the view, in tier rings, the chains round it faded away. Hover anyone for who they are, click for their card, drag and scroll to zoom. Back, Esc or scrolling out comes back to the focus, then to the overview, and scrolling in over a bridge opens its focus, so it is one zoom all the way in. Network Circle's focus is unchanged.
  • No more search pages read for nothing. A scan saves each page as it's read, so Stop loses nothing read (it saved every 10 pages, and up to nine read pages were lost and read again). An automatic round that reaches pages an earlier run read ends there, and the next round opens the first page not read yet instead of clicking through them again.
  • Finished lists start again only when you ask. Your connections, a Breach company and Read all mutuals no longer go back to page 1 by themselves once every page is read (up to 100 more search pages for your connections): their button says Read again from page 1 then.
  • Every page you load by hand counts. Previous, going back to a page already read, a reload and the address bar were not counted, and now are, so the window and LinkedIn usage are true.
  • Auto scan's LinkedIn data no longer breaks a page. When LinkedIn's own data gave the list's total, the page failed after it was counted and its people weren't saved; two in a row set a six-hour pause.
  • The scanner plugins are 1.0.12.

2.8.0

17 October 2026

  • 7 added
  • 8 changed
  • 1 fixed

The mutual web, new Map views, focus on Bridge Chains, a cleaner Separation, a replay that shows every dot, and a Network Circle that stays smooth at ten thousand.

Added

  • The mutual web. Every mutual line Sixgree already reads ("Maya Chen, Leo Park & 103 other mutual connections") is now kept as ties: that person was seen with Maya, and with Leo. They come from every card a scan reads (a circle, your connections, a company for Breach) and from any profile you have open in Sixgree's LinkedIn browser, its mutual line read where it already shows. Nothing is opened just to collect them. A tie seen again counts up, with the date it was last seen. A tie means seen together, never close.
  • Past the 1,000. Someone seen with one of your connections is reachable through them, even when that connection's circle scan stopped at LinkedIn's 1,000 or was never run. They show as "seen outside the scan", lighter and with a dashed ring, in Network Circle, Bridge Chains' focus, Separation (marked on the way in) and Breach. They are never counted as scanned, and a circle with only them in it still offers Scan their circle.
  • Known through. A person's card, and each dot in the focus, says how you know them: "Known through: Maya Chen, Leo Park (seen together 3 times, last on Oct 16, 2026)".
  • Show ties. A switch under Network Circle, in Bridge Chains and in the focus draws thin lines between people who were seen together, for the person or circle in hand only, never the whole web at once.
  • Warm doors in Breach. A door whose mutual line named one of your connections says who to ask ("Ask Maya Chen"), counts that as a mutual, and comes first.
  • Read all mutuals. On Breach's Summit and on your S and A tier connections, a button reads the "N other mutual connections" list in full, in rounds of 5 pages, by itself or by hand, with the same breaks, daily limit and push-back as a circle scan. Each person on it becomes a tie. It only ever runs when you press it.
  • Settings → Your data says how big the web is, as what was seen ("1,240 ties from 3,100 cards, 12 profiles and circle scans"), never as if it were complete.

Changed

  • The replay shows your network growing one person at a time. It used to run the calendar in a straight line, so most people arrived in the last few seconds, a scanned circle landed all in one frame, and a recording showed little but the end. Now it plays on a stage of its own over Network Circle, paced by people rather than dates: each one arrives on their own, in the order you connected, your connections dashing out from you along their line and each circle springing out round the person it came through, with S and A named as they arrive. A busy day (a conference, a scanned circle) is slowed down and a quiet year passes in under a second, so no one is skipped. Speeds from 0.25x to 4x, a timeline you can drag that shows the busy stretches, the years, and your job starts and posts, and the options: each person or each circle (a bubble beside its bridge that grows as its people arrive), tier or degree colours, the whole map or a camera that follows whoever is arriving, names on or off, and Loop. Wheel, drag or pinch to look around. A recording is the same replay at the speed you set, a frame for every step, so the video shows each person arriving; it saves on this computer. It stays smooth with 15,000 people on the map, in every look, Daylight included, and fits a phone. Saved pictures and videos are named by this computer's date, not tomorrow's after 8 pm.
  • Network Circle stays smooth at ten thousand dots, and filters apply without starting over. Tapping a tier or degree chip, the Tiers switch, or anything in Filters used to throw the whole layout away and lay it out again from the middle, 30 to 45 seconds of a busy map at 13,000 people. Now everyone already on the map keeps their place, newcomers start beside whoever they came through, and the map settles in about a second; hiding a tier and showing it again puts everyone back exactly where they were. Coming back to the tab, reopening Sixgree, or switching Rings to Degrees and back starts from the layout you left, kept in this browser only. While a big map does settle, each frame writes only the dots and lines that moved enough to see and none off screen, so it settles faster and smoother; a quick run of taps is drawn once, after the last. The look, the forces and the settled shape are the same as before. Filters also groups its controls under On the map, shows how many of everyone are showing, says how many people are at each degree, and has Reset filters, which brings back every tier at the degrees the tab opens with and leaves the Tiers switch as it is.
  • The scanner plugins are 1.0.11.
  • The Map's views: Heat, Flows and Hubs, from one tidy card. The finder, a switch between four views and the legend for the one showing now share one card, with Declutter and From you folded under it. Heat is a smooth density surface of everyone, tier S, S and A, or one degree, with a legend and its densest place a click away. Flows draws the busiest 20, 40 or 80 routes from your connections to where the people in their circles live, as thick as how many, a dash running the way each goes; a route lists who carries it, each with Their reach and Ask for an intro. Hubs ranks places by reach (the people there plus everyone your connections there can introduce you to), rings the top ten as wide as their reach, and lists them in Top places, each a click from the map flying there. The view is remembered in this browser.
  • The chain to someone, on the Map. While a person's card is open, the map draws you, then each person along their chain (the one Network Circle draws), numbered at their places, with a card to step along it, fit it on screen, and ask the right connection for an intro.
  • The Map's dots are a little smaller at the whole-world view, its legends and buttons take the look's own colours, and a view fades in unless the system asks for less motion. Heat is a view now, not a box to tick under the options.
  • Separation, in tiers. The people to connect with are in sections, S to D, each with a header in its tier's colour, how many it holds, its scores and a fold. The tier chips show and hide whole sections, more than one at a time; inside a section the order is the list's own (rank, or the slider's). Each person is a card that reads You → your connection → them, the connection in their own tier's colour whatever the chips show, with one clear next step: Connect, which opens their profile and marks the request sent, or, once asked, a plain "✓ Request sent" in its place, and View profile beside it, both in Sixgree's own browser where they can. No colour stands for two things any more (gold is S's alone, Path is no longer C's green), words read on Daylight, the search box keeps clear of the side buttons, and on a phone the chips wrap instead of scrolling sideways.
  • Bridge Chains opens the same focus as Network Circle. Click a bridge, or rest the pointer on it for 3.5 seconds (a ring fills round it as you wait), and the overview eases in on it and softly blurs while its circle comes forward sharp, with every catalyst's circle chained outward, rings by degree counted along the chain. A bridge you met through another's circle shows the trail to it under the circle, and a press on any step before it brings that one's circle forward. With nothing scanned you see just them and Scan their circle. Esc, Back, a click outside, or scrolling or pinching out eases you back, and their card opens as it does in Network Circle. It replaces the hover preview and the circle opened in place on a click; links to someone's circle open the focus too. Both views share one implementation, so they look and move the same, in every look, Daylight included, and at any width.

Fixed

  • Opening someone's card from the Map's finder no longer leaves the map showing open sea: the map narrows for the card, and the flight to their place is aimed again at the new size.

2.7.0

16 October 2026

Scan in rounds of 25 pages, a tour and Help, and a slider for breaks.

Added

  • Circle scans in rounds. Scanning one person's circle now reads one round of their list, 25 pages (about 250 people) by default or 50, the most, set under Scanner settings → Pages a round. Each round carries on where the last one stopped, automatically or by hand, so the 100 pages LinkedIn shows of anyone's list take 4 rounds, or 2. Never-scanned people start at page 1; Rescan from the start reads a round from page 1 again. The person card, the Scan page and the In progress list say "Round 2 of 4" and offer Scan next round. Breach's company rounds are as they were.
  • Rounds round the dot. In Network Circle, each bridge's dot has a quiet ring of 4 arcs (2 with rounds of 50) in the look's own colours, filling as their list is read: pages read out of their page total, which is their connection count divided by 10 when it's known (100 at most), else 100. Only on your connections who have a circle, or could have one scanned.
  • Break length by hand. Scanner settings → Breaks by hand has a slider for how long each break is, 5 to 120 seconds. It starts at the scan speed's length (5, 10 or 30 seconds), moving it sets your own, and Use pace default goes back to the speed's. The scanner follows it at the next break, even mid-scan.

Changed

  • Lift limits for this session keeps a ceiling. Lifted, scans still stop at 250 search pages in 24 hours, Dev mode's ceiling; before, the lift had no ceiling at all. The Scan page, LinkedIn usage and the notch say so, with how many are left.
  • The scanner plugins are 1.0.10.

Fixed

  • Network Circle's degree chips count what the map draws. The chips under the map, and the Filters grid, counted the degree each person was saved at, so they could read "3rd 0" beside dots in the 3rd's colour. They now count, and show or hide, the degree each person is drawn at along the chain, the 4th to the 6th included, and picking Degrees or the fan turns them all on.
  • An in-app update no longer stops at "ENOENT … mkdtemp". If the temporary folder Sixgree was started with has been deleted since, the update makes it again, or works in /tmp (or the data folder) instead, and the app it reopens is no longer handed that folder.
  • When there's no scanner, buttons now say to install the Auto scan or Manual scan plugin, not that "the scanner files are missing from this install".
  • Signing in, or a security check, in Sixgree's own browser no longer tells you to use "the Chrome window".
  • A short tour at the start. Once the guided setup is done, the map offers a minute's tour: a film first (a marked slot for now, for a later release to fill), then a step for each place, with a ring round the real tab or button: Network Circle and its tiers, focusing on someone's circle, Degrees and Bridge Chains, Separation, Outlink, the Map, Breach, Scan with its breaks, and the LinkedIn tab when its plugin is on. Back, Next, Skip tour and Esc. It's offered once, never while a scan runs, and only has the steps for the tabs you have.
  • Settings → Help. Replay the tour, a switch for hints, short how-tos that each link to the place they're about, what scanning's pace and limits mean (LinkedIn can restrict an account: slow is safer, never a guarantee), the keyboard shortcuts (⌘[ and ⌘] in the LinkedIn panel, Esc, ⌘. and ⌘,), and where your data lives.
  • Hints. A small card the first times you open a place, one at a time, until you press Got it: "Hold the pointer on one of your connections for 3.5 s… to focus on their circle", "Breach: start with Doors", and one for each tab. Never during a scan, never over the tour. Settings → Help turns them all off, or brings back the ones you dismissed. Remembered on this computer, in this window only.

2.6.0

16 October 2026

  • 4 added
  • 2 changed
  • 3 fixed

Breach a company, focus on anyone in your circle, and the LinkedIn panel closes itself when an invitation is sent.

Added

  • Breach, a tab of its own. Pick a company you have no way into, and Breach shows how far in you are: the company as an orb, its people on seniority rings with the top seats at the top, a line into the orb for each of your connections inside, and a breach meter ("Breach 62 · highest seat VP Sales") with milestones for the first door, director, VP and C-suite. The score is graded: mapping the company counts a little, and each person inside counts by their seat, a VP a big jump and C-suite or a founder the most. Companies only for now.
  • Doors, Climb and Summit. People there are sorted into the likeliest to accept (a mutual, a lower seat, recruiters, your city or school), middle managers, and the top seats, and they move up a band as their mutual counts rise on rescans.   • Pages on other local ports can no longer send the app commands. The check that refuses writes from other websites trusted the browser's "same-site" label.   • npm run update — discards the regenerated lockfile, pulls, installs, and tells you to restart. Plain git pull refuses whenever npm has rewritten package-lock.json, which is most of the time on a second machine, and it says so in one line that is easy to miss.
  • A downloadable macOS app. npm run build:app produces Six Degrees.app and a ~70 MB .dmg with a Node runtime inside, so importing a CSV needs nothing else installed — no Node, no clone, no terminal. macOS asks for one approval on first launch because the app is not signed with a paid developer certificate.
  • Clicking a bridge on the Revolver dial now opens that person, instead of only spinning them to the top. The dial also says when it is showing part of something — "12 of 14 bridges", "showing 24 of 59" — and shift with the arrow keys pages through the rest.
  • The profile page shows how much of your network has actually been mapped: a bar for the connections whose circle you have opened, how many keep their connections private, and how many are left — with the remainder given in batches rather than as one long run.
  • An Updates section on the Scan page. Press Check for updates to see what has changed since your copy, then install it — no terminal, no git pull to remember. Nothing is ever checked automatically and nothing about you is sent; it runs the same two git commands you would type. If you have your own edits, it refuses and tells you which files rather than throwing them away.
  • An import for the official connections export (Connections.csv), parsed entirely in the browser and never persisted.
  • A synthetic sample network (150 invented 1st-degree, 598 2nd-degree, 14 bridges) that seeds deterministically, so every view can be explored before importing anything real.
  • An empty state offering the three ways in, replacing a blank first run.
  • npx six-degrees — a single-command launcher on port 6363 with its own data directory.
  • Reference scoring model (scripts/score_new_connections.sql), transcribed to lib/rpc.js.

Added

  • A Scan page that runs the scanner for you. It checks what is missing, installs it, opens the platform so you can sign in, and runs the scan — with the live log on screen. No second terminal, no server to start, nothing to copy and paste.
  • Scanning a company or auto-bridging can now be run from the command line too (--company "Acme", --auto-bridge); they used to exist only behind the old server.

Added

  • The Scan page now covers 2nd-degree mapping as its own step, in batches of 10, 25 or 50 people, with a Stop button. Stopping closes the browser cleanly and keeps everything found so far.
  • --max-bridges caps how many people one run will visit.

Added

  • Choose which tiers the 2nd-degree scan works through. It always resumes where it left off — anyone still without a mapped circle, highest tier first, including people you have connected with since the last run — so there is nothing to remember and nothing to reset.

Fixed

  • The packaged Mac app could ship without part of Next's server runtime, leaving every page working and every API call failing. The rule that keeps build artefacts out of the bundle was matching Next's own dist folder too.
  • Leaving the Bridges view no longer throws. A resize callback could run once after the view had gone and read something that was no longer there.
  • Scan, Profile, Import and Outlink scroll again. A rule that exists so the map can fill the window was applied to the whole app, so on every other page anything below the fold was rendered but unreachable.
  • npm run dev now says when another copy is already serving on one of its ports, instead of silently starting on a different one while your browser shows the old.
  • Resuming a scan no longer re-scans people you had already done. Past 2,000 second-degree records the check for "who is already mapped" was silently returning a partial answer, so finished bridges looked unfinished.

Changed

  • Orbit now draws the shape of your network rather than every person at once. The people whose circle you have opened are large and named, their circle gathers tightly around them, and everyone else is a small dot until you hover or select them. On a 750-person network that is a third fewer things on screen and no photographs to load for anyone but the hubs — so it is quicker, and there is something to look at.

Changed

  • The 2nd-degree scan now defaults to 10 people at a time instead of 25, and says why: during development a real account was temporarily restricted after about 19 in one sitting. Shipping a default above the only number we have measured is a default that can hurt whoever trusts it.

Fixed

  • A photo is only ever saved for one person now. If the same picture comes back for somebody else — which is what happens with the platform's placeholder silhouette, and with anyone the scanner mismatched — it is skipped and they show their initials instead.
  • Second-degree people no longer end up wearing someone else's profile photo. The bridge scanner matched people by the text of their link, and everyone the platform hides (shown with no name) shares that text — so they all inherited the first one's picture and profile. npm run audit:avatars reports any already saved that way, and --fix clears them back to initials without re-scanning anything.
  • Connecting with someone you met through a bridge now registers. They used to stay a 2nd-degree contact forever — the import saw they were already on file and skipped them — so the outreach you actually completed never showed up in your network.
  • Their card now says who introduced them, and keeps saying it after they become a direct connection.
  • The Revolver dial shows every bridge it can fit rather than a fixed twelve — around forty on a normal window — shrinking them as the ring fills and naming only the selected one once names would overlap. It still pages when there are genuinely too many, and says so. It held the twelve with the largest circles and said nothing about the others, so lower tiers looked as though they did not exist — while the filter beside it was counting them.
  • Stopping a run no longer leaves a browser window open behind it. The scanner is started in its own process group and asked to stop rather than killed, so it closes the browser and reports what it managed to do.
  • Auto-bridge no longer stalls on people whose connections are hidden. It notes them, moves on, and does not try them again — they used to reappear at the top of the list on every run, so the same few profiles were retried forever and the feature looked stuck. --retry-private gives them another go; --clear-skips forgets all of them.
  • The wait after a hidden profile is now seconds rather than two minutes. The long pause is for runs that actually read many pages; a hidden profile was a single page view.
  • A hidden or unavailable profile is recognised in seconds instead of costing the full page-load budget twice over.
  • Auto-bridge prints a countdown while it waits, so a pause cannot be mistaken for a hang.
  • The scanner now collects your whole connections list. It was reading only the first ten people: the list sits in its own scroll container, so the page-down it performed never scrolled anything and no further connections ever loaded.
  • Everyone is collected, not just people with a profile photo. Names now come from the profile link rather than being guessed by comparing the start of a name against the URL.
  • Signing in waits for you instead of against you. There is no countdown; close the browser window to cancel. It also detects the sign-in wherever you finish it, including in a second window.
  • --login signs in and exits, so a first run is not a sign-in and a scan at once.
  • A scan that cannot read the page now says so and stops, instead of reporting that you have no connections.
  • Every scan button in the app works again. The buttons on the map, the profile page and the paths page all quietly pointed at a separate server on port 5555 that no longer exists, and told you to double-click a file that no longer exists either. They all run through the app now.
  • Filtering connections by scanned company was silently ignored and returned everything, because that field was missing from the query allowlist.
  • The scanner's packages install into their own environment, so an install can no longer succeed against one Python while the scanner runs on a different one that lacks them.
  • Scanned people are attached to your profile, so a finished scan can no longer leave the app showing an empty network.

Changed

  • The data layer is now local SQLite via Node's built-in node:sqlite. The hosted Postgres dependency is gone: no account, no keys, no service. Runtime dependencies are next, react, react-dom and d3.
  • All database reads go through the server; no browser-side database access remains.
  • Avatars and the scanner's Chrome profile live in ~/.six-degrees, never the package directory.

Security

  • Destructive API routes are open on loopback, require ADMIN_TOKEN as a bearer from any other host, and fail closed when exposed with no token set. The gate keys off the server's own bind address rather than the Host header, which a caller can forge.
  • Cross-site writes are refused on every API route, so a page on another site cannot drive this app through your browser.
  • Values from the database are escaped before rendering; graph tooltips no longer build markup from stored strings.
  • CI fails the build if a credential, an exported CSV, real avatars, or a non-synthetic network snapshot is ever committed.
